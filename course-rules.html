Rules & Regulations

ON THE COURSE

The format, the prize money and the draws are on the Format page. This page is what happens once you are actually stood on the tee — how it is scored, what a drop costs you, and what earns you a zero.

Read it before Friday. Nobody wants a twenty-minute debate on the 4th at Porthmadog.

Learn these five

THE NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. NO GIMMES

Every putt is holed out. Every single one. It does not matter if it is three inches, it does not matter if your partner has already scored, and it does not matter if everyone else has walked off. Putt it in.

2. TWO RECORDS, EVERY ROUND

One player updates Squabbit live. A player from the other team in your tee group keeps a paper card. Both, every round, no exceptions.

3. NO GROUNDING IN BUNKERS

Club does not touch the sand before you swing. Not behind the ball, not in front, not a practice waggle.

4. WHITE STAKES ARE ABSOLUTE

Beyond a white marker is out of bounds. No debate, no local interpretation, no "it was only just over".

5. IF IN DOUBT, HIT A PROVISIONAL

It costs you thirty seconds. Not hitting one can cost you the hole entirely. See below.

THE SPIRIT OF IT

Nobody here is playing for a card that goes to a handicap committee. But there is money on it, there are medals, and the Birdie Club pot is real. Play it properly and there is nothing to argue about afterwards.

Getting it written down

SCORING & RECORDING

THE LIVE CARD — SQUABBIT

One player in each group is responsible for keeping Squabbit up to date as you go. Do it on the tee of the next hole, not at the end of the round when nobody can remember what happened on the 7th.

Signal on these courses is patchy in places. If it will not sync, keep entering and let it catch up.

THE PAPER CARD

A player from the other team in your tee group keeps a paper scorecard for the same group. Deliberately not your own partner, so there are two independent records.

If Squabbit and the paper card disagree at the end of the round, the paper card is the one that settles it.

WHY BOTH

An app that drops out, a phone that dies, or a mis-tap on a par 5 are all more likely than you think across 54 holes. Two records means no round ever has to be reconstructed from memory in the bar.

The wildcard, and how he is counted

THE BRETTINI BONUS

Brett's score does not go on the live leaderboard. During the round, the team he has joined plays and records their own better ball exactly as normal. Brett plays alongside and keeps his own score.

AT THE END OF THE ROUND

The team he joined goes through the card and finds every hole where Brett's points beat both of theirs.

They total up how many points better off they are on those holes. That figure is the Brettini Bonus, and it is added to their cumulative score across the three days.

ALSO RECORD THIS

Note how many holes Brett's score was used on. Not the points — the number of holes.

That number is what settles his payout at the end of the tour under Performance Related Pay. Zero holes, nothing. Nine holes, half a share. All eighteen, a full share. Write it on the card.

BRETT IS IN THE DAILY COMPS

Closest to the pin and closest in three are open to all seven players, every day, including Brett. The bonus system only affects the team scoring, not the daily money.

Nominated fresh each morning

THE DAILY COMPETITIONS

Each morning, the team that is splitting that day nominates one par 3 and one par 5 for the daily competitions. They pick, everyone plays them.

CLOSEST TO THE PIN — THE NOMINATED PAR 3

Nearest the hole from the tee shot. Must be on the putting surface to count.

CLOSEST IN THREE — THE NOMINATED PAR 5

Nearest the hole after three shots. Must be on the putting surface to count.

Mark it, measure it honestly, and tell the group before you leave the green. All seven players are eligible, Brett included.

The one that costs people points

LOST BALLS & PROVISIONALS

THE RULE, IN ONE SENTENCE

If you think the ball might be lost, say so, drop another and play a provisional before you leave the spot. If you do not play a provisional and the ball is not found, you score zero points on that hole.

This is the single most common way a hole gets thrown away on these trips. It takes thirty seconds and one extra ball. On three coastal links courses in a wind, assume you will need it at some point.

Say it out loud

Tell the group you are playing a provisional before you hit it. If you do not announce it, the second ball becomes your ball in play and the first one is dead whether you find it or not.

The one exception

If the ball has gone into a yellow-staked area and all players in the group agree it definitely went in there, you do not need a provisional. Take your drop as set out below and play on with a one shot penalty.

"All players agree" means all of them. If there is any doubt in the group, treat it as a lost ball.

White markers

OUT OF BOUNDS

White stakes and white lines mark the boundary of the course. Beyond them you are out of bounds, and the local rules printed on each club's card apply in full. Read the card at each course — they are not identical.

IF YOU PLAYED A PROVISIONAL

Play the provisional. It becomes your ball, and you have added a penalty shot. Carry on.

IF YOU DID NOT

You have two options: walk back and play again from where you hit it, or take zero points for the hole.

Going back to the tee on a busy Bank Holiday links is not popular. Play the provisional.

WHAT "GOING BACK" ACTUALLY MEANS

You replay from where you hit the last shot, and you add one penalty stroke. So a drive out of bounds means your next shot from the tee is your third. You lose the distance and you gain a shot.

The bit everyone gets wrong

RED STAKES vs YELLOW STAKES

Both are penalty areas. Both cost you one shot. The difference is how many ways you are allowed to get out.

YELLOW — REMEMBER IT AS "BEHIND"

Your relief options all take you backwards. Either replay from where you hit it, or go back on the line.

The line: hole → the point where the ball last crossed the edge → and onwards, as far back as you fancy. You can stand fifty yards back on that line if it gives you a better number.

Typically a stream or pond crossing the fairway in front of you.

RED — REMEMBER IT AS "SIDEWAYS"

Everything yellow gives you, plus the option to drop two club lengths from where it last crossed the edge, no nearer the hole.

Typically a ditch or water running down the side of a hole. Nefyn's card names ditches on the 3rd and 7th.

THE REFERENCE POINT IS THE CROSSING POINT, NOT THE BALL

For a penalty area, everything is measured from where the ball last crossed the edge — not from where the ball ended up. The ball is in the water and it does not matter where.

So work out the crossing point first and agree it with the group. Everything else follows from there.

Measure club lengths with the longest club in your bag, which for everyone here is the driver. Drop from knee height, and the ball must come to rest within the relief area.

Found it, but you would rather not

UNPLAYABLE BALLS

Gorse, whins, a bush, up against a wall, plugged in a bank. You can declare a ball unplayable anywhere on the course except in a penalty area, and only you decide. Nobody can talk you into playing it and nobody can talk you out of it.

It costs one penalty shot, and you have three ways out.

1. GO BACK AND REPLAY

Return to where you played the last shot and hit again. One shot penalty.

2. BACK ON THE LINE

Draw a line from the hole, through where your ball lies, and extend it backwards. You can go back as far as you like along that line and drop anywhere on it.

Usually the most useful of the three — it lets you retreat to a proper yardage rather than dropping two feet into more trouble.

3. TWO CLUB LENGTHS

From where the ball lies, two club lengths in any direction, no nearer the hole.

THE LINE, IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Stand so the flag, your ball and you are all lined up, with the ball between you and the flag. Now walk straight backwards away from the hole. Anywhere along that path is a legal drop.

There is no limit on how far back you go. If you are in the gorse and forty yards back gives you a clean full wedge, take it — one penalty shot for a shot you can actually play beats three hacks sideways.

IN THE GORSE, BE HONEST WITH YOURSELF

On these three courses the gorse is a reload, not a recovery. Conwy in particular is lined with it. Taking the penalty and playing your next from the fairway will nearly always cost you fewer shots than three hacks sideways, and in Stableford a hole you have already lost is worth abandoning quickly.

UNPLAYABLE IN A BUNKER

Options 1, 2 and 3 above all apply, but 2 and 3 must keep you inside the bunker, and it is still one shot.

If you want to get out of the bunker altogether and drop behind it on the line, that is allowed — but it costs two shots.

Links golf, so this will come up

BUNKERS

YOU MAY NOT

  • Ground your club behind or in front of the ball
  • Touch the sand on your backswing
  • Deliberately test the sand to see what it is like
  • Take a practice swing that touches the sand

YOU MAY

  • Remove loose impediments — stones, leaves, twigs
  • Lean on a club outside the bunker to steady yourself
  • Dig your feet in to take your stance
  • Smooth the sand at any time, so long as it does not improve your lie for the next shot

Rake it afterwards. All three courses are links and the bunkers are revetted in places — leaving a mess in a pot bunker on a Bank Holiday weekend is a good way to get remembered.

Playing as a pair

ORDER OF PLAY

In better ball, you and your partner can play in whatever order suits the team. You do not have to go furthest-from-the-hole first, and you can swap the order deliberately to your advantage.

USE IT ON THE GREEN

If your partner is closer to the hole but you want to see the line and the pace, let them putt first. Watch what the ball does, then hit yours.

This is completely legitimate and it is one of the biggest advantages of the format. Most people forget it exists.

USE IT OFF THE TEE TOO

If one of you is on a run and the other is struggling, let the confident one go first and take the pressure off. On a tight driving hole, the man in play first changes what the other one has to do.

Talk to each other on the tee. You are a team.

PACE OF PLAY

Ready golf otherwise. Play when you are ready and it is safe, whether or not you are away. Two of the three tee times are late morning on a Bank Holiday weekend and there will be groups behind us all day.

If a hole has gone completely, pick up. You cannot score on it and the group behind will thank you.
