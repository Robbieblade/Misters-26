THE SULTANS OF SWING

John Sitch (18) · Adam Fermie (24)
Formerly The Honky Tonks

The cowboy hats have gone and the neon has arrived. The name is new, the personnel are not, and this is the pairing with the widest range of possible outcomes on the entire tour.

The argument for them is simple: if John brings his driving and Adam brings the form he has found, they have high-forties better ball rounds in them comfortably. Add a Brettini round on top, with three scores to choose from on every hole, and the first 50-point round in Misters history is a realistic prospect. That would end the tournament on its own.

The argument against them is equally simple. Ham and egg only works if one of them is the ham. When they both score badly on the same holes at the same time, the numbers collapse — as they did at Silves last year, where 36 points effectively ended their tournament on day two.

What is not in doubt is the atmosphere. They bring the vibes, the drinking on the course and the late-night hot tub sessions. The real question for the other five is whether you can follow them into that water and still play golf the next morning.

The golfer

The biggest hitter in the group and the one whose ball you turn to watch. Genuinely tour-level distance when he connects, and a fearlessness in recovery that means no lie is ever quite unplayable — though he is often only in that position because of what happened one shot earlier.

Portugal was a solid rather than spectacular week: 98, 92, 102, with the 92 at Silves the second-best round anyone posted that day. Two birdies, joint-most in the field alongside Rob, but only nine pars — the fewest of anyone except Adam. That is the profile in one line: he makes things happen, in both directions.

He is also the only player in the field whose handicap has not moved. Everyone else has been cut. Whether that reflects a settled game or a year without much golf is a question worth asking on the first tee.

STRENGTHS

  • Distance — the biggest driver in the group by some margin
  • Recovery play, and the nerve to attempt it
  • Thrives in front of a crowd and in the closing holes
  • A remarkable ability to play well on day three of a trip

WEAKNESSES

  • The misses are very wide when the timing goes
  • Short game and putting are not where his attention goes
  • Streaky — capable of losing the rhythm and not finding it again mid-round
  • Only nine pars in 54 holes; the card runs hot and cold

The man

The ringleader. Last to leave the bar, first to suggest one more, and reliably capable of a 300-yard drive the following morning after a night that should have ruled him out entirely. Managing hangovers is, unironically, one of his golfing skills.

This year he has fallen for the American dream and spent time stateside, returning with international plans. He has also, like Sam, taken up padel — same question, same uncertainty about what it does to a golf swing.

“A man who is dangerous in every sense.”

— Anonymous teammate

Dragon Tour outlook

The Sultans live or die by his driver. Porthmadog and Conwy give him room to open the shoulders. Nefyn does not — The Point has out of bounds marked on both the 11th and 12th and the sea for company, and that is precisely where a big miss becomes a lost hole. If he plays the percentages for one round only, that is the round.

The golfer

The most interesting case in the field, because the reputation and the recent evidence point in opposite directions. Portugal was, statistically, the hardest week anyone had: 329 gross, 79 best ball points, four pars in 54 holes and eight triple-bogeys or worse. The 115 at Silves was the highest round of the tournament and took the Honky Tonks out of contention.

And yet. He was the most consistent player in the field by score range — just nine strokes between his best and worst rounds, the tightest of anyone — and he still contributed 31 points at Salgados, the second-highest individual round of day one. He delivered exactly what he had, every single day.

Since then the game has moved. He has been shooting gross eagles, stringing pars together, and the handicap has come down three shots. If the version of Adam who has turned up in recent months is the one who gets on the plane — or in the car, in this case — the Sultans have a genuinely different proposition.

STRENGTHS

  • Game management — knows when to attack and when to take the fat of the green
  • Rarely in serious trouble off the tee when the driving is behaving
  • Short game inside 50 yards keeps the bad holes to bogey
  • Unflappable. Laughs off a double bogey, which is worth more than it sounds
  • The most improved player in the field on recent form

WEAKNESSES

  • Only four pars across the whole of Portugal — the ceiling has to lift
  • Performance drops as the pint count rises, and he is at peace with that
  • Plays it safe a little too often; sometimes misses the chance to attack
  • The old wayward driving is a distant memory rather than a dead one

The man

The gag man and the group’s morale officer. Capable of making friends with an entire bar inside ten minutes, and of charming free drinks, taxis or questionable directions out of total strangers. Cannot swim, which makes him the designated non-lifeguard of a group that spends a lot of time near water.

He has spent the year travelling, and not only for work. The phrase doing the rounds is that he has been becoming a better him, which is either sincere or the setup for a punchline, and with Adam it is usually both.

“At his best, a real danger on the course. At his worst, a danger to anyone on adjacent fairways.”

— Playing partner

Dragon Tour outlook

If the improvement is real, this is the story of the tour. He is off 24, which means shots on every hole with a stroke index of 6 or better and two on the toughest six — including Conwy’s 5th, where he gets two shots on a 435-yard par 4 rated stroke index 1. A player in form with that many shots is dangerous. Porthmadog, the hilliest walk, is the one round where the legs might matter.
