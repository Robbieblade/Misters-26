Saturday 29 August · The big night

BARMOUTH, NOT HARLECH

Harlech is lovely. Harlech is also two decent pubs, a castle and everyone in bed by half eleven. On the one Saturday night of the tour, on an August Bank Holiday weekend, that is not what we came for.

Barmouth is twenty-five minutes away on the train. It has a proper seafront, a cluster of pubs within two minutes of each other, live music, somewhere open past midnight and — should things escalate — an actual nightclub.

THE HEADLINE

The Royal has two separate live acts on at 8:30pm. Ben Maitland upstairs, Giff & Neil downstairs. Two rooms, two bands, Bank Holiday Saturday. That is the difference between seven blokes sat round one table and seven blokes stood up in a busy pub.

THE CRAWL

The Royal ★★★★★

20:00 – 22:15. The main target. Two bars, sport, pool and snooker, and two live acts on Bank Holiday Saturday — Ben Maitland upstairs, Giff & Neil downstairs from 20:30.

For seven men in their early forties this is the sweet spot. People stood up rather than sat down eating, two rooms to move between, and other groups in.

Get there before 20:30
Ffynnon Cocktail Bar

19:00 – 20:00. The opener. A newer cocktail place rather than a traditional pub. Open until around 21:30, so it is a starter not a base.

One drink, then move
The Last Inn ★★★★

22:15 – 23:00. Recent local reviews say it is packed on Saturday nights. Historic pub with a rock face and a spring running through the back bar. There is a fair chance this ends up the favourite bit of the night.

Worth the look even if The Royal is going well
Barmouth Bar & Grill

23:00 – 00:30. The late one. Listed open until 00:30 on Saturdays, which is later than most of the town. This is what stops the night dying at eleven.

The bridge to after midnight
Tal Y Don Hotel

Backup. In the same cluster. Worth a look if The Last Inn is quiet or The Royal has emptied.

Reserve option
The Club

00:30 onwards. Saturdays only, 10pm – 3am. An actual nightclub with a dance floor and a DJ. Reports vary from "quiet but we had the dance floor to ourselves" to "the music was terrible".

Do not make this the objective of the night. But at half twelve, after six hours drinking, there is no chance this group walks past it. Bank Holiday Saturday is the one night of the year it might genuinely be busy with holidaymakers.

TRANSPORT

The train out

Harlech to Barmouth on the Cambrian Coast line. Around 25 minutes, and one of the great short train journeys in Britain — it runs along the estuary and crosses Barmouth Bridge over the Mawddach. Do it in daylight.

The last train back — and why we are not getting it

There is a 22:48 from Barmouth, arriving Harlech around 23:14.

That means leaving at exactly the point Saturday night gets going, on the only Saturday of the tour, on a Bank Holiday weekend. It would be an appalling lack of commitment to the Misters and it will be remembered for years.

We are not getting the 22:48.

The way home

Barmouth to Harlech is about 11 miles, roughly 22 minutes by road. The single most important thing on this page is that we book this in advance. Seven men trying to find two taxis in Barmouth at 2:30am on Bank Holiday Saturday is not a plan, it is a walk.

Costs are indicative. Late night on a Bank Holiday will carry a premium, and some firms charge more after midnight. Split seven ways the minibus is comfortably the best value at roughly £7–10 a man.

TAXI FIRMS — RING THEM THIS WEEK, NOT ON THE NIGHT

Local firms covering Harlech and Barmouth are small operations and the good ones get booked up on Bank Holiday weekends. Search "Harlech taxis" and "Barmouth taxis", and ask specifically for an 8-seater for a 02:30 pickup from Barmouth town centre to Harlech.

Worth also asking the owner at Aelfor — local hosts usually have a number for someone reliable, which beats anything you will find online.

SORT THIS BEFORE FRIDAY

1. BOOK THE MINIBUS

8-seater, Barmouth town centre to Harlech, 02:30. This is the one that ruins the night if it is not done.

2. CHECK THE TRAIN

Confirm the Saturday evening departure from Harlech. Bank Holiday timetables are not the same as normal Saturdays.

3. EAT SOMETHING

Sunday is Conwy, 11:03, and it is the round that decides the tournament. Get food in Barmouth early rather than discovering at 1am that nobody has eaten since the turn.

ONE HONEST WARNING

Conwy is the longest card of the tour at 6,476 yards, it holds stroke index 1, 2 and 3 in the last five holes at the 17th and across the front nine, and it decides the trophy, the medals and the Birdie Club pot.

Everyone will say they will be fine. In Portugal the final day was where it was won and lost. Do with that what you will.
